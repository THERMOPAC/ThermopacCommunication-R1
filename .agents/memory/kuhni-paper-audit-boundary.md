name: Kühni paper audit boundary
description: Missing equation extraction must not be mistaken for absence of a literature mixing correlation.
---

Audit original equations before claiming the stored papers contain no predictive axial-mixing route. Asadollahzadeh (2017), model section Eq. 9, explicitly invokes a continuous-phase axial-mixing correlation; the original Markdown extraction omitted its algebra, but the primary PDF has now been recovered. Its reference is Kumar–Hartland (1992), for rotating-disc and asymmetric rotating-disc columns, so transfer to Kühni must be assessed rather than silently accepted.

**Why:** Earlier advice conflated an unimplemented or unreadable correlation with nonexistent evidence, and incorrectly suggested pilot measurements were a prerequisite for all pre-pilot calculations.

**How to apply:** Recover and visually verify the source equation and definitions, distinguish correlation extrapolation from a universal constant, and label uncertainty. Laitinen (2019) Table 3 provides continuous-phase dispersion values for its own experiments; the reported 20–30% backflow is a specific CFD observation, not a transferable default. Inspect primary typography: its Eq. 10 prints a suspicious resistance denominator, and Table 2 interfacial-tension units conflict with the measurement text. Do not silently transcribe either.

Direct Kühni mixing lead: Steiner, Kumar and Hartland (1988), “Determination and correlation of axial-mixing parameters in an agitated liquid–liquid extraction column,” DOI 10.1002/cjce.5450660208. Its publisher abstract explicitly reports both-phase Kühni dispersion measurements and improved continuous-phase backmixing correlations. This confirms a relevant source exists, not that its exact formula or applicability has been verified. Tahershamsi et al. (2016), IJOGST article 41600, cites it; the retrieved full text does not attribute a mixing equation to Ingham. DOI 10.1021/acs.iecr.6b04353 concerns pulsed/nonpulsed disc-and-doughnut equipment, not a Kühni correlation.

Primary source typography now matters more than the old extraction gap. The
Asadollahzadeh IJCChE mass-transfer PDF Eq. 9 (printed p.153) visibly prints
1.05×10^-2 multiplying NDR/Vc linearly, a barred Vc in the LHS denominator,
and a trailing “e” after the geometric groups. The supplied screening formula
instead uses 0.0126, plain Vc, and no trailing factor. Do not conflate these.
The -2 belongs to 10^-2, NOT an exponent on NDR/Vc. Nomenclature confirms N
in s^-1, but does not resolve every special Eq.9 symbol.

**Why:** Text extraction and a read-only source report each misread the typeset
equation. A successful HTTP download recovered the PDF where HTTPS retrieval
had failed. The mass-transfer article is distinct from the same authors'
CER&D capacity/Vk study; its rejection cannot establish the mixing equation.

**How to apply:** Inspect the retained primary PDF and equation crop under
research/sources/kuhni-mass-transfer before asserting source equivalence.
Engineer-approved screening expressions must be labelled separately from
source-verified published transcription when these discrepancies remain.

Original mixing reference is Kumar–Hartland (1992), DOI
10.1002/cjce.5450700112, not the Steiner (1988) cross-check. Its publisher
abstract reports RDC mixing up to 20% above ARD, but that does NOT justify
replacing only 0.0105 by 0.0126. The 2017 nomenclature defines V as superficial
and N as s^-1 but does not separately define Vbar or the trailing e. Neither
“interstitial velocity” nor “Euler's number” is established by that source.
