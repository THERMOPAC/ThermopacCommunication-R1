Conclusions
Published experimental data on dispersed-phase hold-
up for eight different types of differential and stagewise
extraction columns are brought together into a unified
correlation. The effect of external agitation is accounted
for in terms of the power dissipation per unit mass.
The
dimensionless correlation contains
seven
predictor
groups, some of which can be redundant for a particular
type of column due either to lack of variation in the
parameters involved or to peculiar hydrodynamic condi-
tions. The average error
of prediction on the entire data
set is 18.1%, which is better than that achieved by most
authors in attempting to correlate their own
data. The
highest error
is 22.7% for rotating disk and asymmetric
rotating disk columns and the lowest 14.1% for spray
columns.
The errors
for pulsed perforated-plate and
packed columns are
19.0 and 18.3%, respectively.
The predicted values of hold-up may
be used to
calculate the slip velocity to an
even
better accuracy
than that achieved in the hold-up.
