Audit of `.agents/outputs/garthe-2005-review/paper.txt` (line references; printed page numbers shown in text):

**Scope/materials and geometry.** Kühni tests use agitated compartments height `hc=50 mm`, relative free stator cross-section 40% (p.63, lines 3444-3450); appendix geometry includes agitator diameter range `dA=41–85 mm`, stator diameter `ds=47.5–83.2 mm`, column diameter `DC=72–152 mm` (p.85, lines 4604-4633). Characteristic-velocity data cover `nR=50–250 1/min`, drop diameter `d=0.9–6.4 mm`, 143 points, relative error 10.7% (p.85, lines 4604-4633). Systems are toluene(d)/water, butyl acetate(d)/water, toluene(d)/acetone/water, butyl acetate(d)/acetone/water, plus tridecanol/water and cumene/hexane vs water/23 wt% sucrose in the correlation comparison (p.85, lines 4604-4633). Physical-property table at 20 C: densities 863–998.8 kg/m3, viscosities 0.566–1.163 mPa·s, interfacial tension 10.96–34.31 mN/m, acetone distribution coefficient 0.843/0.933 (p.50, lines 2734-2747). No RRBO or NMP appears in the paper’s experimental systems; these are low-viscosity benchmark fluids, not viscous RRBO/wet-NMP.

**Characteristic velocity.** The recommended Kühni correlation is (5.6), p.85, lines 4590-4595:
`vchar,o/vo = 1 – 1.669·NP^(-3.945) – 2.807·[d/(DC−dA)]^1.336 – 1.159·(hc/DC)^2.049 + 2.1·phi_s^1.032` (OCR layout puts exponents above terms). Power number (5.7), same page/lines 4597-4602: `NP = 1.08 + 10.94/ReR + 257.37/(0.5 ReR^1.5)` (the displayed denominator is `0.5 ReR^1.5`; retain source notation). ReR is rotor Reynolds number `ReR=nR dA^2 rho_c/eta_c` (p.28, lines 1642-1644). Authors explicitly say geometry, drop diameter and energy input must be included and reject Weiss because it omits geometry/drop size; Fang omits drop diameter (p.84, lines 4508-4517). Kühni energy is much higher than rotating discs; circulating cells occur even at 100–200 rpm (p.83, lines 4474-4482).

**Swarm velocity/holdup definitions.** Dispersed holdup `hd=Vd/(Vd+Vc)`; relative swarm/slip velocity `vrs=vd/hd + vc/(1−hd)` with superficial phase velocities vd, vc (p.28, lines 1651-1664; eq. 2.37). Transformed swarm velocity: `vs=vrs(1−hd)=vd(1−hd)/hd+vc` (eq.2.38, p.28, lines 1663-1668). Richardson–Zaki: `vs/vo=(1−hd)^n`, `n=f(Reo)` (eq.2.39, p.29, lines 1676-1690); cited n regimes include 4.65, 4.35/Reo^0.03, 4.45/Reo^0.1, and 2.39 (figure 2.11, p.29, lines 1695-1729). Drag/swarm model: `cd,s/cd,o=(1−hd)^−4.65` (eq.2.40, p.30, lines 1734-1745), hence `vs/vo=[cd,o(Reo)/cd,o(Res)](1−hd)^4.65` (eq.2.41, p.30, lines 1754-1763), with laminar `(1−hd)^4.65` and turbulent `(1−hd)^2.325` limits (eqs.2.42–2.43, lines 1765-1771). Holdup inversion: eq.2.45 printed quadratic (p.31, lines 1804-1808), and simpler `hd=vd/(vs+vd−vc)` (eq.2.46, lines 1810-1816). These are generic/rigid-particle-derived; no Kühni/RRBO/NMP-specific fitted swarm exponent is supplied.

**Power/energy.** Kühni power enters characteristic velocity through NP in (5.6). The paper gives `NP` correlation above and rotor Reynolds definition, but does not provide a separately validated RRBO/NMP power draw or scale-up law. The correlation is fitted/validated over the geometry, rpm and drop-size ranges above, with 143 points and 10.7% relative error (p.85, lines 4604-4633).

**Breakage.** Modified Weber number used in breakage probability eq.(6.3): `Wemod = [rho_c^0.8 eta_c^0.2 dM^1.8 dA^1.8 / sigma] · 1.6[(2pi nR)^1.8−(2pi nR,crit)^1.8]` (p.99, lines 5299-5309; OCR formatting confirms powers 1.8). `nR,crit` is the speed at first split and its full fitted expression is eq.(6.5), p.99, lines 5312-5319. Kühni constants for eqs.(6.3)/(6.5): C1=1.63e−3, C2=0.48, C3=3.05, C4=0.13, C5=1.21e−2 (table 6.4, p.99, lines 5331-5338). Kühni breakage experiments: `nR=50–250 rpm`, mother drops 2, 3, 4 mm; higher rpm not studied/practically uninteresting (p.100, lines 5347-5357; figure p.101 lines 5372-5400). Butyl acetate breaks more than toluene; acetone lowers interfacial tension and increases breakage (p.100, lines 5359-5363). Two-Kühni tests at 150 rpm show high breakage; ~90% reported around 175 rpm (p.103, lines 5494-5542). Daughter-drop correlation (6.6): `ndd=2+C1(dM/dstab)^C2−1`; Kühni parameters C1=0.03, C2=2.45 (p.105-106, lines 5608-5618 and 5657-5667). It can support a DPBM drop-size model only if stable diameter/coalescence inputs are independently supplied; it is not a direct RRBO/NMP drop-size prediction.

**Mass transfer.** Kühni extractor behavior is qualitative/performance data, not a transferable coefficient equation. Turbulence/circulating cells, low axial mixing and large interfacial area give high transfer (p.144-145, lines 7798-7808). Increasing rpm 100→150 nearly doubles equilibrium stages per length for butyl acetate/acetone/water; rates are higher than other tested extractors but decrease near flooding (p.145, lines 7805-7821). Low maximum throughput is attributed to relatively small drops and low characteristic velocities (same lines). The experiments measure equilibrium stages per length vs total throughput, using the two benchmark systems; no RRBO/wet-NMP `kLa`, `k_c`, `k_d`, diffusivity correction, viscosity correction, or mass-transfer scale-up equation is supplied.

**Flooding/capacity/diameter scaling conclusion.** The paper discusses generic flooding theory and says flooding is where a small flow increase causes infinite dispersed holdup (p.32, lines 1836-1855), but its explicit flooding illustration/model is for packed columns (Mersmann diagram; swarm exponents 1–3, p.129, lines 6904-6923). Kühni-specific capacity is only the qualitative observation that performance falls near flooding and maximum throughput is low (p.145, lines 7810-7821); no Kühni fitted flooding/capacity envelope or usable RRBO/NMP capacity limit is given. Although (5.6) contains DC, dA, ds-related geometry and thus a characteristic-velocity geometric scaling within its calibration range, it is not a column-diameter scale-up law: no RRBO/NMP diameter validation, holdup/flooding scaling, or viscous-fluid extrapolation is supplied.
