## Abstract

The effects of the density ratio _η_, the viscosity ratio _λ_, the inverse viscosity number _Nf_, and the Eötvös number Eo on the shapes and terminal velocities of a drop rising in [stagnant liquids](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/stagnant-liquid) are studied numerically using a front tracking method. It is found that mild variation of _η_ barely changes the drop shape and [terminal velocity](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/terminal-velocity) while keeping the other non-dimensional parameters fixed, whereas _λ_, _Nf_ and Eo significantly affect the [drop dynamics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemical-engineering/drop-dynamics). With the increase of _λ_, _Nf_ or Eo, the drop becomes either more dimpled or more crescent. The flow details inside and around the drop are also investigated. A recirculation zone is observed for small _λ_ or large Eo, and it becomes larger with the decrease of _λ_ and increase of Eo. In addition, the qualitative and quantitative relationships between Fr, the non-dimensional [terminal velocity](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/terminal-velocity) of the drop, and _λ_, _Nf_ and Eo are proposed based on the simulation results.

## Introduction

Drops and bubbles widely exist in liquid–liquid or gas–liquid two-phase systems. Their dynamics is of fundamental importance to a variety of engineering applications such as the process of solvent extraction, underground transport of pollutants \[1\], hydrocarbon production in oil wells and its transportation in pipelines, and heat exchanger. And specifically their shapes and terminal velocities are crucial in modeling particle motion and heat/mass transfer through the interface. As a consequence, quite a number of experimental and numerical investigations have been conducted to study the dynamics of drops/bubbles. Wairegi and Grace \[27\] experimentally investigated the shapes of drops moving in immiscible liquids, and reported different shape families including cap-shaped, crescent-shaped, biconcave, and toroidal drops. Kojima et al. \[13\] also observed toroidal drop and its breakup. Koh and Leal \[12\] investigated the stability of oblate and prolate drops translating through a quiescent fluid and found that a tail or wake formed behind the drops. Their experimental results were found consistent with the results of their own numerical simulations \[11\] and those by Pozrikidis \[18\]. Wesselingh \[28\] reviewed the theory developed by Clift et al. \[3\] and revealed that the terminal velocity of drops increases with surface tension. Other experimental work by Grace et al. \[6\] and Myint et al. \[16\], Myint et al. \[15\] also demonstrated the variation trends of the shapes and terminal velocities of drops moving through stagnant liquids. Grace et al. \[6\] proposed a shape regime map for drops and bubbles in terms of the Eötvös, Reynolds and Morton numbers. Taylor and Acrivos \[24\], Harper and Moore \[8\], Dandy and Leal \[4\], and Bozzi et al. \[2\] investigated the dynamics of a deformable drop at different ranges of the Reynolds number. Taylor and Acrivos \[24\] extended the theoretical analysis on the deformation and drag of a drop at a small non-zero Reynolds number based on some assumptions and previous experimental work. Harper and Moore \[8\] studied the flow inside and around a drop at high Reynolds numbers theoretically, including the spherical vortex flow inside the drop and the potential flow outside. Dandy and Leal \[4\] found that the shape of a drop tends to be a spherical cap as the Weber number increases at lower Reynolds numbers, whereas it becomes more disk shaped as the Weber number increases at higher Reynolds numbers. Bozzi et al. \[2\] proved that the curvature in the rear region of a drop increases with decreasing the Reynolds number. Other numerical studies on this subject include Helenbrook and Edwards \[9\], Fakhari and Rahimian \[5\], Ohta et al. \[17\]. Helenbrook and Edwards \[9\] presented the drag force on a drop in a gaseous system, and proposed an improved model for drag estimation by incorporating the effects of both the drop deformation and the internal circulation. Fakhari and Rahimian \[5\] concluded that the deformation rate would be enhanced by increasing the Eötvös number, and different breakup modes, such as bag breakup, shear breakup, and multimode breakup, were observed in their numerical study. In the investigation conducted by Ohta et al. \[17\], it was believed that the Morton number, the Eötvös number, and the viscosity ratio are the primary governing parameters associated with drop dynamics. However, more comprehensive studies on the effects of these parameters on the drop dynamics are needed to understand the underlying physics, especially to understand the effect of each individual governing parameter.

The dynamics of drops is determined by a group of non-dimensional parameters including the density ratio _η_, the viscosity ratio _λ_, the inverse viscosity number _Nf_, and the Eötvös number Eo, which are defined as:η=ρdρs,λ=μdμs,Nf=ρs(ρs-ρd)gD3μs,Eo=(ρs-ρd)gD2σwhere _ρs_ and _ρd_ are the density of surrounding liquid and drops, respectively, _μs_ and _μd_ are the dynamic viscosity of surrounding liquid and drops, respectively, **_g_** is the gravitational acceleration, _D_ is the initial spherical drop diameter, _σ_ is the surface tension coefficient. Another parameter which is also often used is the Archimedes number defined as Ar=Nf2.

The Froude number Fr, the Reynolds number Re _T_, and the Weber number We _T_ are usually employed to represent the terminal velocity of drops. In the analysis of the steady state results, these numbers are defined asFr=UT(ρs-ρd)gDρs,ReT=ρsUTDμs,WeT=ρsUT2Dσwhere _UT_ refers to the terminal velocity of drops.

This paper aims to systematically investigate the effects of the density ratio _η_, the viscosity ratio _λ_, the inverse viscosity number _Nf_, and the Eötvös number Eo on the dynamic behavior of a drop rising in initially stagnant liquids, especially on the steady shape and terminal velocity of the drop. A front tracking method based on the work by Unverdi and Tryggvason \[25\] is employed in this study, which has been extensively validated for bubbles, Taylor bubbles \[10\], and Taylor droplets rising in tubes filled with another liquid \[20\].

In this paper, numerical simulations of two dimensional bubble rising in the presence of electrohydrodynamic forces are presented. The physical properties of the bubble and the background fluid are adjusted to resemble an oil-water system. The numerical technique utilized to discretize the governing equations is the Lagrangian Incompressible Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (ISPH) method. A single bubble is subjected to an electric field using a leaky dielectric model under different values of Reynolds, Bond and electrical Capillary numbers. The results show that the bubble elongates in the direction of the electric field forming a prolate shape. The increase in the values of Reynolds and electrical Capillary numbers enhances prolate deformation of the bubble, but raising the Bond number reduces the prolateness of the bubble. The interaction of a bubble pair is also investigated for various configurations. If the bubbles are placed such that their centroids are vertically in-line, they tend to merge due to the initial prolate deformation.
