**Verdict for RRBO/NMP (Eo/X=33.6; Mo≈2.86e-9, log10 Mo=-8.54): pre-pilot extrapolation only—not source-validated.** The strongest directly relevant, clean liquid–liquid shape dataset/correlation (Myint–Hosokawa–Tomiyama) covers Mo but only Eo≤9.3. **Key equations / definitions**
1. Dimensionless definitions for fluid particle in quiescent liquid (Myint et al.):
   Eo = g d²|rho_c-rho_d|/sigma; Re = rho_c V_T d/mu_c; Mo = g mu_c^4|rho_c-rho_d|/(rho_c² sigma³); We = rho_c V_T²d/sigma; kappa=mu_d/mu_c. d is volume-equivalent spherical diameter; c/d continuous/dispersed; V_T terminal velocity.
2. Wellek (via Myint’s reproduction) non-oscillating drops in **contaminated immiscible liquids**: aspect ratio E=(b1+b2)/(2a)=1/[1+0.091 We^0.95]. For low/moderate-viscosity systems: E=1/[1+0.163 Eo^0.757]. This is a shape closure, not Cd(Re).
3. Myint clean-drop shape correlation: Ta=Re Mo^0.23; E=1-0.0487 Ta-0.0289 Ta². Exact stated domain: -11.6≤log10(Mo)≤-0.9; 1.5e-2≤Re≤8.5e2; 1.7e-2≤Eo≤9.3; 7.4e-3≤Ta≤3.6; 0.1≤kappa≤100. Thus Mo and potentially Re>50 are in range, but Eo=33.6 is 3.6× its upper bound.
4. Shape variables: E=(b1+b2)/(2a), gamma=2b2/(b1+b2), where a is major semi-axis and b1/b2 are bottom/top distances to it. Myint reports surfactants reduce E and break fore–aft symmetry especially at low Mo; kappa weakly affects E but affects gamma.
5. A separate liquid–liquid single-drop numerical study uses eta=rho_d/rho_s, lambda=mu_d/mu_s, Nf=rho_s sqrt[(rho_s-rho_d)gD³]/mu_s, Eo=(rho_s-rho_d)gD²/sigma, Re_T=rho_sU_TD/mu_s, We_T=rho_sU_T²D/sigma. Its stated range is 0.65<eta<0.79, 0.1<lambda<10, 5<Nf<100, 16<Eo<47; it models silicone-oil drops rising in glycerol-water. It is geometrically relevant to Eo=33.6 but not an RRBO/NMP validation and exact final correlations were inaccessible.

**Candidate audit / conflicts**
- **Grace / Clift–Grace–Weber:** Grace map is qualitative regime guidance, not by itself an exact liquid–liquid Cd(Re) closure. The accessible shape source attributes a fluid-particle-in-water kappa relation to Grace, but the rendered equation is ambiguous; do not implement from it without original-page verification.
- **Ishii–Zuber:** candidate is for vapor drift/bubbly-droplet multiphase modeling; no primary liquid–liquid deformed-drop equation/range recovered. Do not claim RRBO/NMP support.
- **Rodrigue / Loth:** Loth 2008 is a high-quality review, but explicitly says no quantitative theory exists for pure-drop deformation or drag; it notes no substantial kappa dependence over 0.8<mu*<2.4 in the cited data. It supports the evidence-gap verdict, not a deployable closure.
- **Feng–Michaelides:** search evidence identifies drops falling in a **gas** (density ratio ~1000); not transferable to liquid–liquid RRBO/NMP absent explicit justification.
- **Bubbles:** Tadaki/Vakrushev and Tomiyama-type branches found are bubble closures. They cannot be used as direct drop closures; Myint explicitly says bubble correlations cannot accurately evaluate drop E.
- **Contamination conflict:** Thorsen et al. (1968) experimentally found Hu–Kintner not generally valid for highly purified systems and stressed extreme contamination control. Wellek’s relation is explicitly contaminated. Surface-active RRBO/NMP composition therefore must be measured/governed, not assumed clean/contaminated.
